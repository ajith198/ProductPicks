A good multi-tool is one of those items you don't think about until the moment you need it — a loose tent stake, a fraying paracord end, a screw that backed out on a camp stove, a stubborn zip tie on a car battery. The problem is that "multi-tool" covers everything from a $10 keychain gadget to a $230 titanium-handled do-it-all, and the tool count printed on the packaging ("21-in-1!") is one of the least useful numbers for deciding which one is actually right for you. Here's what to actually look at, and a few picks across price points.

## What actually matters

**Plier-based vs. knife-based.** This is the biggest fork in the road. A plier-based multi-tool (Leatherman Wave+, Gerber Suspension-NXT) opens like a pair of pliers, with the other tools folding out from the handles — great for camp repairs, wire work, and anything that needs grip strength. A knife-based multi-tool (Victorinox Swiss Army knives) is built around a folding blade with smaller tools tucked into the handle — lighter, more pocket-friendly, but no pliers, so it's weaker for mechanical repair work. Decide which category you actually need before comparing tool counts.

**Which tools you'll actually use.** Needle-nose pliers, a real knife blade, wire cutters, a bit driver or screwdriver set, and scissors cover the vast majority of camping and everyday situations. Saw blades, awls, and rulers are nice-to-haves that rarely get used. If a listing's "18 tools" is mostly redundant flathead sizes and a ruler, it's padding the count, not the utility.

**One-handed opening and locking blades.** On the tools you'll use one-handed — the main knife blade especially — check whether it opens with one hand (thumb stud or hole) and whether it locks open. A blade that can fold back on your fingers mid-cut is a real, common injury on cheap multi-tools.

**Steel and build quality.** Full-size multi-tools from established brands (Leatherman, Gerber, Victorinox, SOG) use decent stainless steels (420HC, 154CM, or similar) that hold an edge reasonably well and resist rust. Ultra-cheap unbranded "27-in-1" tools sold for $15-$20 often use soft steel that dulls fast and pliers that flex under real pressure — fine as a stocking-stuffer novelty, not something to rely on.

**Weight and carry method.** Full-size plier tools run 7-9 ounces and usually come with a belt sheath — noticeable in a pocket, fine on a belt or in a pack. Compact/keychain tools run under 3 ounces and disappear in a pocket but sacrifice leverage and tool size. Match this to whether the tool is living in your pocket daily or riding in a glovebox or camp kit.

**Warranty.** Leatherman and Gerber both back their multi-tools with generous limited warranties (Leatherman's is 25 years) covering material and manufacturing defects — worth factoring in for a tool you expect to carry for years.

## Our picks

**[Gerber Dime](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Gerber+Dime+multi-tool&tag=AMAZON_AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER)** — around $25. A keychain-sized multi-tool with spring-loaded needle-nose pliers, wire cutters, a fine-edge blade, scissors, a small file, a flathead, a package opener, and tweezers, all in about 2.2 ounces. It's the pick if you want something that genuinely disappears on a keychain or in a bag pocket rather than a full-size tool you have to remember to carry. The trade-off is tool leverage — the pliers are small and better for fine work than serious mechanical tasks.

**[Gerber Suspension-NXT](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Gerber+Suspension-NXT&tag=AMAZON_AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER)** — typically in the $40-$55 range. A full-size, plier-based 15-tool multi-tool that undercuts Leatherman's flagship pricing while covering the same core bases: spring-loaded pliers, wire cutters/strippers, a knife blade, screwdrivers, and a bit driver, with a pocket clip and included sheath. It's a sensible middle ground for someone who wants a real plier tool without spending premium-tier money, and Gerber's fit and finish on this line is a step up from their budget offerings.

**[Leatherman Skeletool CX](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Leatherman+Skeletool+CX&tag=AMAZON_AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER)** — typically around $80-$100. A stripped-down, lightweight take on the plier-tool concept: pliers, wire cutters, a 154CM stainless knife blade with a pocket clip and one-handed opening, a bit driver, and a bottle opener — deliberately leaving out the tools most people never use, which keeps it around 5 ounces. This is the pick for someone who wants Leatherman build quality but finds full-size tools too bulky for daily pocket carry.

**[Leatherman Wave+](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Leatherman+Wave+Plus+multi-tool&tag=AMAZON_AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER)** — typically in the $100-$130 range depending on retailer and sales. Widely considered the benchmark full-size multi-tool: 18 tools including outside-accessible blades and scissors (usable without fully unfolding the pliers), replaceable wire cutters, a saw, and a full driver set, backed by Leatherman's 25-year warranty. If you want one tool that covers camping repairs, home fixes, and general EDC without compromise, this is the one most gear reviewers keep coming back to — the trade-off is weight (about 8.5 ounces) and a $100+ price tag.

**[Victorinox Huntsman](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Victorinox+Huntsman+Swiss+Army+knife&tag=AMAZON_AFFILIATE_LINK_PLACEHOLDER)** — typically around $50-$65. If you don't need pliers at all, a classic Swiss Army knife is worth considering: a locking main blade, scissors, a saw, a can/bottle opener, and a few screwdrivers, all in a slim, pocket-friendly package that's noticeably lighter than any plier tool. It won't help with wire work or gripping a hot pot, but for pure EDC and light camp tasks it's hard to beat for the price and pocket feel.

## How to decide

- **Want it to disappear in a pocket or on a keychain and mostly do fine work?** Gerber Dime.
- **Want real pliers but don't want to pay premium-brand pricing?** Gerber Suspension-NXT.
- **Want Leatherman quality in the lightest, most pocket-friendly plier tool?** Skeletool CX.
- **Want one tool to genuinely cover camping repairs and daily use without compromise, and don't mind the weight or price?** Wave+.
- **Don't need pliers at all and want the lightest, most classic option?** Victorinox Huntsman.

## Verify before you buy

- Confirm current price and any active bundle/sheath inclusions on the retailer's listing — these move around with sales.
- Check the exact tool list on the specific model page; brands sell near-identical variants (with/without saw, different blade steel) under similar names.
- If one-handed opening matters to you, check the blade-opening mechanism in the listing photos or description before buying — not every model in a lineup has it.
- Look at the sheath/carry method included (belt sheath vs. pocket clip vs. none) if how you'll carry it matters to your decision.
