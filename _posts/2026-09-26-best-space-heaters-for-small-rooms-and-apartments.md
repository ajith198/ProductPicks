As temperatures start dropping, a lot of apartment dwellers and anyone with a chronically cold bedroom or home office start shopping for a space heater rather than cranking up central heat for one room. The category looks simple from the outside — it's just a box that gets hot — but the differences between heater types and safety designs actually matter a lot, especially in a small space where the heater sits close to furniture, bedding, or where you sleep.

This guide covers what actually matters when choosing a space heater, then breaks down specific picks across budget tiers.

## What actually matters when choosing a space heater

**Heater type: ceramic vs. oil-filled radiator vs. radiant.** Ceramic (convection) heaters use a fan to blow air over a ceramic heating element, warming a room's air relatively quickly and evenly — good for rooms you're actively using right now. Oil-filled radiators heat a sealed reservoir of oil that radiates heat slowly and steadily with no fan noise; they take longer to warm a room but hold heat well after they shut off cycling, and their housings run cooler to the touch, which is often considered a safer pick for bedrooms or homes with kids and pets. Radiant (infrared) heaters warm people and objects directly rather than the air, which is efficient for spot-heating a desk or armchair, but the heating element itself can glow red-hot, so keeping flammable objects (curtains, bedding, paper) well clear of it matters more than with the other two types.

**Room size vs. wattage.** Most plug-in space heaters top out at 1,500 watts (the standard limit for a household circuit), which is generally enough for a bedroom or small office (roughly 150-300 sq ft depending on insulation and ceiling height) but not a whole open-plan living area. If you're trying to heat a larger or drafty room, look at BTU ratings and reviews that specifically test larger spaces rather than assuming more expensive means more powerful — most models in this price range cap out at the same wattage.

**Safety features.** This is the category where corners genuinely shouldn't be cut. Look for tip-over shutoff (the heater powers off if knocked over), overheat protection (shuts off if internal temperature gets too high), and ideally a cool-touch housing so it's not a burn hazard to bump into. A shutoff timer is also worth having so the heater doesn't run all night unattended if you fall asleep.

**Noise.** Ceramic fan heaters produce continuous fan noise, which is a real consideration for a bedroom — some models are noticeably quieter than others at the same wattage, and this is worth checking in reviews before buying if you're a light sleeper. Oil-filled radiators are silent since they have no fan.

**Controls and extras.** A thermostat that actually holds a set temperature (rather than just low/medium/high heat settings) saves on running the heater at full blast constantly and helps with energy costs. Remote controls, digital displays, and oscillation (for wider, more even coverage) are conveniences rather than necessities, but worth factoring into price comparisons.

## Before you buy: a quick checklist

- Confirm the heater has tip-over shutoff and overheat protection — don't compromise on this regardless of price.
- Match wattage/BTU claims to your actual room size; don't expect a 1,500W unit to heat an open floor plan.
- If you're a light sleeper, check reviews for fan noise specifically before buying a ceramic model for a bedroom.
- Never run an extension cord to a space heater if avoidable — plug directly into a wall outlet per manufacturer instructions.
- Prices on all of these move with seasonal demand — check the current listing before assuming the price quoted here still holds.
