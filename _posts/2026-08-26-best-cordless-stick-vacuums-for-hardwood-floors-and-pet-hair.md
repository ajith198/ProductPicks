Cordless stick vacuums have mostly replaced the corded upright as the everyday vacuum for a lot of households — especially ones with hardwood or LVP floors and a shedding dog or cat. The category ranges from sub-$100 models sold mostly through Amazon and TikTok Shop to $300-$500 flagships from Dyson and Shark, and the honest truth is that the suction numbers printed on the box (measured in Pa, or "air watts," or "kPa") are the least useful way to compare them. What actually separates a vacuum you'll use daily from one that ends up in the closet is battery runtime under real load, how well the brushroll handles long pet hair without tangling, and whether it's light enough to hold overhead for stairs and upholstery without your arm giving out.

## What actually matters

**Battery runtime on the box is almost always measured on the lowest suction setting — real-world runtime on a setting strong enough to lift embedded pet hair from carpet is usually much shorter.** A vacuum rated for "40 minutes" often only delivers 10-15 minutes on its highest suction mode, which matters if you're doing a whole house rather than a quick spot clean. If pet hair or carpet is a regular part of your cleaning, plan around the max-mode runtime, not the headline number, and check whether the battery is removable/swappable (a second battery extends real cleaning time without waiting for a recharge).

**Brushroll design determines whether long hair actually gets picked up or wraps around the roller until you're cutting it out with scissors.** Look specifically for language like "anti-tangle," "self-cleaning brushroll," or a comb/guard design built into the roller — these use small teeth or a rubber wiper that continuously strips hair off the bristles as it spins. A plain bristle brushroll without this feature will work fine at first and then get progressively worse at picking up fine debris as hair accumulates around it, usually within a few weeks in a home with a shedding pet.

**Weight and balance matter more once you factor in overhead use.** A vacuum's published weight is usually for the full stick configuration on the floor; the same vacuum can feel much heavier when held up for stairs, curtains, or car interiors because the motor and dustbin (the heaviest parts) sit at the top, away from your hand. Lighter overall weight and a motor positioned closer to the handle both make a real difference for anyone doing more than quick floor passes.

**HEPA-grade filtration matters most for allergy-prone households, not for suction power.** A sealed HEPA filtration system keeps fine dust and dander from blowing back out of the exhaust while vacuuming — worth prioritizing if anyone in the house has pet allergies or asthma, less critical if you're just clearing visible hair and crumbs.

## Before you buy: a quick checklist

1. **Check max-mode runtime, not just the headline number** — most published runtimes are measured on the lowest suction setting, and real cleaning on carpet or heavy pet hair usually needs a higher setting.
2. **Confirm the brushroll has anti-tangle or self-cleaning tech** if you have a shedding pet — a plain bristle roller will need manual hair removal within weeks.
3. **Weigh the vacuum's balance, not just total weight**, especially if you'll use it overhead for stairs, ceiling fans, or car interiors.
4. **Price out a spare battery** if the vacuum will need to cover a large home in one session — swappable batteries vary a lot in cost between brands.
5. **Confirm current price and bundle contents on the listing before buying** — cordless vacuum pricing shifts often with sales and model refreshes, and the ranges above are approximate at the time of writing.

None of this needs to be complicated: figure out how much floor space and pet hair you're actually dealing with per session, prioritize real battery life on a suction setting you'll actually use over the headline runtime number, and pick the brushroll design that matches your pet situation. Any of the four picks above will outperform a corded vacuum for daily use — the real differences come down to weight, runtime, and how well each one resists hair tangling over time.
