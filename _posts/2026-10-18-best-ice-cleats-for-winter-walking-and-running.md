A bad patch of ice on a driveway or sidewalk is one of the most common ways people get hurt in winter, and it's also one of the cheapest problems to solve. Slip-on traction devices stretch over your regular shoes or boots and add grip in seconds — no separate winter boots required. But "ice cleats" covers a wide range of products, from soft rubber coils meant for a quick walk to the mailbox up to aggressive steel spikes meant for icy trail running or hiking, and picking the wrong one means either sliding anyway or destroying your kitchen floor the first time you forget to take them off indoors.

This guide covers what actually separates these designs, then breaks down real picks by use case.

## What actually matters when choosing ice traction devices

**Coil vs. spike construction.** The two basic designs work very differently. Coil-style traction (thin steel springs wrapped around the sole, the classic Yaktrax look) spreads grip across the whole foot and works well on packed snow and light ice on mostly flat ground — sidewalks, parking lots, driveways. Spike-style traction (short stainless steel or tungsten-carbide spikes set into a rubber harness, the Kahtoola MICROspikes/NANOspikes style) bites much harder into glare ice and uneven, sloped terrain, but feels more aggressive and less comfortable on bare pavement or indoors.

**Spike count and material.** More spikes per foot generally means more consistent grip, but the material matters as much as the count. Heat-treated stainless steel spikes (common on hiking-oriented microspikes) hold up to repeated hard use on rock and ice. Tungsten-carbide spikes (common on running-oriented designs) are smaller and lighter, trading some aggressiveness for a more natural stride.

**Intended activity.** This is the single biggest factor. A product built for a slow walk on a shoveled sidewalk doesn't need the aggressive bite — or the added weight and stride disruption — of a product built for running or hiking on unmaintained, icy trails. Buying the hiking-grade option for sidewalk use usually just means an uncomfortable walk and faster wear from pavement contact that the spikes weren't designed for.

**Harness fit and sizing.** Nearly all of these size by shoe size range (e.g., women's 6-9, men's 8-11), not a universal one-size-fits-all stretch. A harness that's too loose can shift or spin on your shoe mid-stride; too tight and it's a hassle to get on and off. Check the sizing chart against the actual shoes or boots you plan to wear them with, since cleats sized for a running shoe may not stretch comfortably over a bulkier winter boot.

**Pavement vs. ice-only use.** Walking any spiked traction device on bare concrete or indoors for more than short stretches dulls the spikes faster and can be slippery on tile or hardwood (the spikes don't grip smooth hard surfaces the way they grip ice). Coil-style designs tolerate occasional bare pavement better than spike designs do.

## A simple decision framework

- **Sidewalks, parking lots, dog walks, commuting on cleared-but-icy pavement** → Yaktrax Walk or Yaktrax Pro.
- **Road or sidewalk running through a snowy winter** → Yaktrax Run for packed snow, or Kahtoola NANOspikes if you regularly hit glare ice.
- **Trail running on icy, unmaintained terrain** → Kahtoola EXOspikes.
- **Hiking on steep, icy, or mixed snow-and-rock trails** → Kahtoola MICROspikes.

## Verify before you buy

- Match the size chart to the actual shoes or boots you'll wear them with, not just your normal shoe size — bulkier winter boots may need a size up.
- Take spiked traction off before walking on tile, hardwood, or other smooth indoor flooring; the spikes don't grip those surfaces and can be slippery or damaging.
- Coil-style traction wears down faster with regular bare-pavement contact — if most of your route is cleared pavement with only occasional icy patches, that wear adds up over a season.
- Store traction devices dry between uses; rust on steel spikes and coils shortens their lifespan.
- Prices shift with winter seasonal demand, so compare current listings before buying rather than relying on any price quoted here.
