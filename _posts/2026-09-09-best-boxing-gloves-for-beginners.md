Walk into any sporting goods aisle or scroll an Amazon search for "boxing gloves" and you'll find everything from $20 novelty sets to $300 pro-endorsed leather. For a beginner who's about to start hitting a heavy bag or pads at a gym, most of that range is noise — the things that actually matter are weight (in ounces), closure type, and how much real padding is over the knuckles. Here's what to look at, and a few picks across price points.

## What actually matters

**Weight (oz), not brand.** Boxing gloves are sized by ounces of padding, not hand size the way shoes are. For general beginner training — bag work, pad work, and light technique drills — 14-16 oz is the standard recommendation, since more padding protects both your hands and your training partner's face during sparring. If you're mostly doing solo bag or mitt work and want something lighter, 12 oz is a reasonable starting point, but most gyms will ask you to have at least 14 oz on hand before doing any partner work. Competition gloves (8-10 oz) are a different category entirely and not what a beginner should buy first.

**Closure type: velcro vs. lace-up.** Velcro-strap gloves are the practical choice for almost every beginner — you can put them on and take them off by yourself, with no training partner needed to tie you in. Lace-up gloves lock the wrist more securely and are what you'll see in competition, but you need someone else to lace and tape them, which makes them impractical for a solo gym-goer. Start with velcro; switch to lace-up later if you start sparring competitively.

**Padding layers and material.** Look for multi-layer foam (often described as "layered foam" or a two-tone foam construction) rather than a single dense foam block — layered foam absorbs impact more evenly and lasts longer before flattening out. Genuine or synthetic leather ("PU leather") both hold up fine for training use; full grain leather costs more and lasts longer under heavy daily use, but isn't necessary for someone training a few times a week.

**Wrist support.** A wide, secure wrist strap that wraps well above the wrist bone matters more than most beginners expect — a weak strap lets the glove flop around and increases the chance of a wrist tweak on a hard punch. This is one of the bigger differences between budget and mid-range gloves.

**Hand wraps are not optional.** Gloves alone don't stabilize your hand and wrist joints — hand wraps go underneath every pair of gloves listed here for any real bag or pad work. Budget roughly $10-15 for a pair of cotton or semi-elastic wraps if you don't already have them; it's a separate purchase from the gloves themselves.

## Verify before you buy

- Confirm the exact oz weight on the specific listing — the same glove model is often sold in multiple sizes (10 oz, 12 oz, 14 oz, 16 oz) under one product title.
- Check your gym's glove requirements before buying if you plan to spar; some require a specific weight or brand.
- Budget separately for hand wraps if you don't already own a pair — they're not included with any glove listed here.
- Read recent buyer reviews on the listing for stitching/seam durability complaints, which can vary between manufacturing batches even for the same model.
