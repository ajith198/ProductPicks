Once you're driving to trailheads or group rides instead of just riding from your front door, you need a way to get the bike there without folding down seats or wedging a frame into a hatchback. The confusing part isn't picking a brand — it's picking a *mount type*, because hitch, trunk, and roof racks solve the problem in genuinely different ways, with different costs and different vehicle requirements. This guide is based on published testing and comparisons from outlets like GearJunkie, Outdoor Gear Lab, and Switchback Travel, plus manufacturer spec pages — we haven't personally tested every rack listed, and we'll flag where that matters.

## What Actually Matters

### Mount Type: Hitch vs. Trunk vs. Roof

**Hitch racks** attach to a trailer hitch receiver (1.25" or 2") on the back of your vehicle. They're the most stable at highway speeds, the easiest to load since the bike sits low, and most platform-style hitch racks tilt down for trunk/hatch access even with bikes loaded. The catch: you need a hitch receiver installed first, which is an extra cost (often $150-$400 installed) if your vehicle doesn't already have one.

**Trunk racks** strap to the trunk lid, hatch, or rear bumper with straps and hooks — no hitch required, which makes them the cheapest and most universal option. The tradeoffs are more bike movement at highway speed, blocked rear-hatch access while loaded, and a real risk of paint rubbing where the straps and arms contact the car if you don't use the included padding correctly.

**Roof racks** mount bikes on top of the car, usually on existing roof rails or a crossbar system. They're the only type that leaves your trunk and hitch receiver free, and they keep bikes out of exhaust grime, but you're lifting a bike overhead every time, and garage/carport clearance becomes a real concern — low entrances and drive-thrus are a common story in rack reviews.

### Frame Contact

Older hitch and trunk racks clamp the bike's frame or top tube directly, which can scratch paint or stress carbon frames. Newer "platform tray" hitch racks instead cradle the wheels and use an arm that hooks over the tire, touching the wheel rather than the frame — better for carbon bikes, oddly-shaped frames, and e-bikes with integrated batteries. If you or anyone in the household rides a carbon or step-through frame, a wheel-tray design is worth the extra cost over a clamp-style rack.

### Weight Capacity and E-Bikes

Standard hitch and trunk racks are usually rated for 35-40 lbs per bike, which is fine for most road and mountain bikes but not for a 50-70 lb e-bike. E-bike-rated racks use a heavier frame, a ramp or lower loading height, and a higher per-bike weight rating — check the spec sheet rather than assuming any rack works, since overloading a standard rack is a real failure point reviewers call out repeatedly.

### Receiver Hitch Class

If you're going the hitch-rack route, the rack's "1.25-inch" or "2-inch" spec refers to the hitch receiver opening size, not the bike capacity directly — though in practice, 2-inch receivers are standard on most 2+ bike racks because they're rated for more tongue weight. A dealer or a trailer-hitch installer can tell you which class your vehicle has or needs.

## A Few Things to Verify Before You Buy

- **Confirm your hitch receiver class (1.25" vs. 2")** before buying a hitch rack — the rack's hitch-end spec has to match, and an adapter adds cost and reduces stability.
- **Check the per-bike weight rating against your actual bike's weight**, especially for e-bikes — overloading is one of the most common rack failures reported in reviews.
- **Measure garage, carport, and drive-thru clearance** if you're considering a roof rack — a loaded roof rack adds real height that's easy to forget about until it isn't.
- **Use all the included rubber padding on a trunk rack**, and recheck strap tension after the first few miles — straps settle and loosen.
- **Look up your specific trunk/hatch shape against the rack's compatibility list** — trunk racks are universal in theory but some hatchback and SUV shapes need longer arms than the base kit includes.
