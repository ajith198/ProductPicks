Heating one room (or one bed) with an electric blanket costs a fraction of running a space heater or turning up the thermostat, which is why they've stayed a practical winter buy even as whole-home heating has gotten more efficient. But "electric blanket" actually covers two pretty different products — a couch-sized heated throw for one person on the sofa, and a full bed-sized electric blanket meant to go under your top sheet all night — and the features that matter shift depending on which one you need.

This guide covers what actually matters in either category, then breaks down real picks across budget tiers.

## What actually matters when choosing an electric blanket or heated throw

**Throw vs. full bed blanket.** A heated throw (typically 50"x60" or so) is designed for a couch or chair, for one person, for a few hours at a time, and usually isn't meant to be slept under all night. A bed-size electric blanket (twin through king) is designed to go under your top sheet and run overnight, with safety features built around that longer, unattended use case. Buying a throw to heat a bed, or a bed blanket to drape on a couch, usually works fine functionally — just know which one you're actually ordering.

**Single vs. dual control.** If two people share a bed and want different temperatures, you need a dual-control (sometimes called "dual zone") blanket, which ships with two separate controllers so each side of the bed heats independently. Twin and full-size blankets are typically single-control only (one person, one controller); queen and king sizes usually come with two controllers as standard. Throws are almost always single control since they're a one-person product.

**Auto shut-off.** This is a genuine safety feature, not a nice-to-have — it's what keeps a blanket from running hot for hours if you fall asleep with it set high or it gets bunched up under a pet. Most bed blankets auto-shut-off around 10 hours; most throws shut off sooner, often around 2-4 hours, since they're not meant for all-night use. Don't buy one without this feature.

**Fabric.** Fleece and "microplush"/sherpa blends are the most common — soft, warm without feeling heavy, and generally machine washable (check the care label; some require air-drying the wiring-containing layer and can't go in a dryer). Faux-shearling and quilted cotton options exist too and tend to feel a bit more substantial, at a small weight and price premium.

**Controller type.** Basic analog dial controllers (low/medium/high, sometimes numbered 1-10) are cheaper and simpler. Digital controllers with a preset timer and an LCD display cost more but make it easier to dial in and remember your actual temperature setting instead of guessing at a dial position each night.

**Corded vs. cordless (battery) throws.** Most electric blankets and throws plug into a wall outlet, which is fine for a couch or a bed near an outlet. A smaller number of throws run on a rechargeable battery pack for genuine portability (a patio, a camp chair, a car) — useful for a specific use case, but they run for a few hours per charge and cost more for the convenience.

**Washability and wire flexibility.** The heating wires are now almost always thin and flexible enough that these are washer-safe (gentle cycle, cold water, no bleach, air dry or low-heat tumble per the label), but always double-check the specific model's care instructions before the first wash — a handful of older or cheaper designs still recommend spot-cleaning only.

## A simple decision framework

- **Couch, one person, occasional use** → a basic heated throw (Sunbeam-tier). Don't overspend on bed-blanket features you won't use.
- **Bed, sleeping alone, want simple and cheap** → a single-control twin/full electric blanket in the same fleece/microplush category.
- **Bed, shared, want independent temperatures** → a dual-control queen or king blanket (Biddeford-tier for budget, Beautyrest-tier for a softer feel and digital control).
- **Portability matters more than price** (patio, camping, car) → look specifically for a battery-powered cordless throw rather than a standard corded one.

## Verify before you buy

- Confirm the size matches your bed (twin/full/queen/king) or intended use (throw vs. bed blanket) — listings for the same product line often span multiple sizes with different control setups.
- Check whether the listing includes one controller or two before assuming dual-zone control.
- Look for a stated auto shut-off time and don't buy a blanket without one.
- Read the actual care label for washing instructions rather than assuming — some lines are fully machine washable, others want air-drying only.
- Prices on all of these move with seasonal sales (especially heading into winter), so compare current listings across retailers rather than relying on any price quoted here.
